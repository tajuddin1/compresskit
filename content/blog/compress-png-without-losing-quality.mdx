PNG is excellent for sharp graphics and transparency, but photo-like PNGs can be huge. The goal is not maximum crushing — it is the smallest file that still does the job.

## When PNG is the right format

Keep PNG when you need:

- Transparent backgrounds
- Sharp UI icons or logos
- Screenshots where text must stay crisp

## When another format is better

If your “PNG” is actually a photograph, convert to JPG or WebP. You will usually save more size with less visible damage than fighting PNG alone.

Use the [Image Converter](/image-converter) when transparency is not required.

## A practical PNG workflow

1. Confirm you truly need PNG
2. Crop empty space
3. Resize to final dimensions
4. Compress with CompressKit’s [PNG compressor](/compress-png)
5. Compare the preview carefully around edges and text

## Transparency note

CompressKit keeps transparency when you stay on PNG output. Converting to JPG removes transparency and fills those areas with white.

## Target sizes for forms

Some portals still ask for PNG under 100KB or 200KB. Try target-size compression, but accept that detailed transparent graphics may not hit exact numbers without looking soft.

## Bottom line

Use PNG for the jobs it is good at. For photos, switch formats. For true graphics, resize first and compress second.
