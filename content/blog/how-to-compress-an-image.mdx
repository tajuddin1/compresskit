Large images are one of the easiest ways to slow down a website or fail an upload form. The good news: you can often shrink a file dramatically with little visible change.

## Start with the right format

Photos usually compress best as **JPG** or **WebP**. Graphics with sharp text or transparency often need **PNG**, though PNG files can stay larger.

If you do not need transparency, converting a PNG photo to JPG or WebP can be the biggest win before you even touch quality settings.

## Choose a sensible quality level

Think of quality as a dial, not a pass/fail switch:

- **High quality** keeps fine detail for portfolios and product shots
- **Balanced** is a strong default for blogs and social posts
- **Maximum compression** is useful when a form demands a tiny file

On CompressKit, try Balanced first. If the preview still looks good, you can go lower. If edges look soft, move back up.

## Resize before you crush quality

A 4000px photo displayed at 800px is carrying unused resolution. Resize to the dimensions you actually need, then compress. You will usually save more size with less visible damage.

## Use target size tools carefully

Need about 100KB for a portal upload? Target-size compression is helpful, but exact sizes are not always possible. Detailed images simply need more data. Aim for “close enough and still readable,” not mathematical perfection.

## A simple workflow

1. Crop or resize to the final display size
2. Pick JPG/WebP for photos, PNG when transparency matters
3. Compress with Balanced settings
4. Compare the before/after preview
5. Download only when the result still looks right

## Privacy tip

Prefer tools that process images in the browser. With CompressKit, your files stay on your device during compression, which is especially useful for resumes, ID-style photos and unpublished product shots.
