Choosing an image format is less about trends and more about the job: photo, graphic, transparency or maximum compatibility.

## JPG

**Best for:** photographs, gradients, most social uploads

JPG uses lossy compression that is excellent for real-world photos. It does not support transparency. For blogs, ecommerce galleries and hero photos, JPG is still a dependable default.

## PNG

**Best for:** logos, UI graphics, screenshots with text, images that need transparency

PNG keeps sharp edges well and supports alpha transparency. The downside is size—photo-like PNGs can be huge. If your “PNG” is actually a photo, converting to JPG or WebP usually helps.

## WebP

**Best for:** modern websites that want smaller assets

WebP often beats JPG and PNG on file size at similar visual quality. It can also support transparency. Compatibility in current browsers is strong, which makes WebP a smart choice for performance-minded sites.

## A practical decision guide

- **Photo for a website:** WebP or JPG
- **Logo with transparent background:** PNG or WebP
- **Strict older compatibility:** JPG for photos, PNG for graphics
- **Upload form with a tiny limit:** compress first; format choice still matters

## Conversion is part of optimization

Sometimes the right move is not only compressing, but converting. A transparent marketing graphic may stay PNG. A smartphone photo exported as PNG should probably become JPG or WebP before you spend time fighting the quality slider.
